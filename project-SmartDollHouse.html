Smart Doll House

ArduinoSensorsRemote Control

Overview

A wooden doll house wired up with an Arduino Uno and a dozen different sensors and outputs to make it behave like a real smart home — motion-activated outside lighting, a working doorbell, automatic climate control, and a garage door and room lights you can operate from an IR remote.

The problem

This was a project from a robotics class I used to attend. The house itself and all the sensors were provided as part of the class kit — my job was to write all of the code that tied everything together and made the house actually behave "smart."

What I built

The project breaks down into three main systems, all running on a single Arduino Uno:

Lighting & doorbell. A PIR motion sensor watches the front yard and switches on the outside LED when it detects movement, while a photoresistor separately turns that same light on once it gets dark enough — so the porch light responds to both motion and ambient light. A button wired up as a doorbell triggers a buzzer alarm when pressed. The bedroom and living room each have their own LED that can be toggled independently from the IR remote.

Climate control. A temperature sensor sits in the "kitchen" right underneath a small fan, with an LCD screen displaying both the current temperature and the target temperature the house is trying to reach. An RGB LED shows the system's status at a glance — it lights up blue when the fan is actively cooling, and red whenever the target temperature is set higher than the current reading (i.e. the house wants to warm up). The target temperature is adjustable in 1-degree steps from the remote.

IR remote control. An IR receiver inside the house listens for signals from a handheld IR controller, which acts as the main interface for the whole system. Pressing OK drives a servo motor that rotates 90° to open or close the garage door, while UP and DOWN nudge the target temperature by one degree at a time. Buttons 1 and 2 toggle the bedroom and living room LEDs on and off respectively.

Remote control layout

Outcome

The finished house responded to motion and light automatically, let visitors "ring" a working doorbell, kept its own temperature readout in view on the LCD, and could be fully controlled — lights, garage door, and target temperature — from a single IR remote. It was a great hands-on intro to tying multiple sensor types and outputs together on one microcontroller.
